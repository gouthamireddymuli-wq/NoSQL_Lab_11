Lab Report: Findings & Conclusions
Why Monotonic Keys Cause Hotspots
  • In a ranged sharded collection, MongoDB owns consecutive key ranges per chunk.
  • Every new document with ts = now() always lands in the highest chunk, which always lives on one shard until the balancer migrates it.
  • The balancer can migrate only after a chunk split; with high insert rates, chunks split and migrate cannot keep up.
  • Result: ~95% of writes route to Shard A, starving Shard B of work and saturating Shard A's disk and CPU.
  • Read queries for recent data also fan out to Shard A only, negating horizontal scale.
When a Ranged Key Still Makes Sense
  • Time-series archiving: If most queries are date-range scans and writes are batch (not continuous), a ranged date key enables efficient sequential scans with minimal cross-shard joins.
  • Zone sharding: Pair a ranged date key with zone sharding – assign recent date ranges to fast NVMe shards and old ranges to cold storage shards.
  • Data lifecycle: When the workload is "insert once, read later" (logs, audit trails) and you drop old shards by date, range sharding with TTL indexes simplifies management.
  • Low write rate: At <100 insertions/sec the balancer keeps up with chunk migrations; hotspots are transient.
Key Takeaway: Use hashed shard keys when write throughput and even distribution matter most. Use ranged shard keys when sequential scan efficiency and data lifecycle management matter more than write parallelism. Never use a bare monotonically-increasing key for a high-throughput write workload.
